Generated by `python -m benchmark.fleet.capacity` from `results/load-*.json` (name segments `sweep`, `herd`, `par`, `pginsert`) and the profiles. Do not edit by hand.

Measured on 8 vCPU, 16 GB, `cpu_model: DO-Regular` (a shared-CPU DigitalOcean droplet with slow cores), locust and the collector on one host over loopback, 1,670-finding bundles, p95 < 2 s. The figures are for this box class (see the CI comparison below).

## Method

Open arrival: each host enrols and posts at a random offset within its interval, so the offered load is a steady λ posts/s. Each post is a 1,670-finding bundle. Every run lasts 300 s and is repeated with two seeds. The rule: λ_max is the largest swept rate at which both seeds have p95 below 2 s and a sound measurement, with no higher rate failing; the band's upper edge is the lowest rate where any seed fails or loses requests. Little's law (hosts / (interval + mean latency) against the achieved rate) decides whether requests were lost. It cannot be checked when a host's interval exceeds half the run, so the host count follows the rate: 60 hosts at 0.5 posts/s (120 s interval), 100 at 1 (100 s), 250 at 2 (125 s), 300 at 3 (100 s), 500 at 4 and above. The first SQLite and Postgres passes at 2 posts/s used 500 hosts, a 250 s interval; their `measurement_defect` is `None`, so they are listed as excluded and the 250-host rows decide that rate. The capacity tables come from unprofiled runs at the shipped ingest parallelism (2 slots on both backends). Profiled runs carry asyncio debug-mode overhead and their latencies are not capacity numbers.

## F23: Postgres ingest cost per row

The profile at `04c2650` (`profiles/postgres-after`) shows 16,103 statements per ingest, against 16,996 at `c330937` (`profiles/postgres-before`). The statements are one INSERT per findings row and per framework-view row through the psycopg pipeline, plus one foreign-key check per inserted row. Time inside psycopg's `wait` is 66.4 % of sampled wall time before and 68.8 % after. The single-ingest service time is the Postgres p50 at the lowest swept rate, where nothing queues: 1.1 to 1.3 s. Two slots divide that, so Postgres saturates below the SQLite rate and meets no swept p95 target on this box.

Proposed fix, out of scope for this branch: bulk-load findings with `COPY` or batched inserts, and defer the foreign-key checks to the end of the transaction. Neither is tried here.

## Against the CI runner

The load-ratio CI job runs the same harness on a GitHub `ubuntu-latest` runner (`results/ci-baseline-*.json`), 1 post/s, 60 hosts, 1,670-finding bundles. Both backends meet p95 < 2 s there with room to spare:

- postgres: runner p95 710 ms (60 hosts); this box p95 4100 to 4700 ms (5.8 to 6.6 times slower).
- sqlite: runner p95 150 ms (60 hosts); this box p95 730 to 760 ms (4.9 to 5.1 times slower).

So the λ_max figures describe this box class. They are not a limit of the collector on faster cores, and one CI rate is not a sweep.
