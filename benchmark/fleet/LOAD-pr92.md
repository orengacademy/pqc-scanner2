Box: 8 cores (DO-Premium-Intel), 31 GB, local loopback; locust and the collector share one host, so these numbers are not a network result.

Locust drives simulated agent hosts at the collector (uvicorn plus the database, same host). Pass means ingest p95 under 2 s. Arrival is `staggered` (each host enrols and sends its first bundle at a random offset in the first minute, steady hosts/60 s) or `herd` (every host enrols and posts as it spawns: a 10 s ramp at 50 hosts/s for 500 hosts). The box was shared with unrelated work, so the load average column matters when reading a row. Postgres rows ran against a local `postgres:16-alpine` container.

**Summary.** Target not met. At 500 hosts (one 300-finding bundle per host per minute) SQLite p95 is 8.6 s with all
880 requests answered, and Postgres requests time out at 60 s.

Below 500 the rows are not monotone in host count and the two seeds disagree:
- SQLite passed (p95 < 2 s) at 30, 60 and 120 hosts in every run. At 90 hosts p95 was 3.9 s in seed 1 and 0.76 s in
  seed 2; at 250 hosts 11 s and 1.7 s. The largest count that passed in both seeds is 120.
- Postgres passed at 30 (one run), at 60 and 120 in one seed of two, and never at 90 or 250.
- Box load average (start to end of run): SQLite seed 1 10 to 18, seed 2 15 to 22; Postgres seed 1 11 to 18,
  seed 2 20 to 31. SQLite seed 2 ran on a busier box and was faster at 90 and 250 hosts, so load does not explain the
  gap. Each row has 60 to 480 requests.

So capacity at p95 < 2 s is a band, not a number: between 60 and 250 hosts per collector on SQLite (120 the largest
that passed twice) and at most about 30 to 60 on Postgres.

At 500 hosts on Postgres the collector stored 581 scans while the clients saw 240 succeed; 581 is a lower bound (counted
right after locust exited). The server keeps working on requests the client abandoned at 60 s.
