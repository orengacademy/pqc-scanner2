## Starting facts (read from the PR #92 artefacts before any new run)

- Every PR #92 load row ran on this workstation at a 1-minute load average between 10 and 31 on 8 cores (the `box load` column of `benchmark/fleet/LOAD.md`). The load average is 28 at the time of writing. None of those latencies is a clean measurement of the collector.
- The collector serves ingest through `asyncio.Semaphore(INGEST_PARALLELISM)` with `INGEST_PARALLELISM = 2` (`src/pqcscan/daemon/fleet.py`). Maximum ingest throughput is therefore 2 / s_service. At 500 hosts the harness offered 8.3 posts/s; it achieved 7.4 rps on SQLite (s_service about 0.27 s) and 3.6 rps on Postgres (about 0.55 s). Offered load above that rate queues without bound until the agent's 60 s timeout. That is the 61 s Postgres figure.
- The PR #92 arrival model was "every host posts once per 60 s" (the collector's per-host refill). 500 hosts at that cadence is 8.3 posts/s. FLEET.md's deployment model is a daily timer with a 2 h random delay (`packaging/fleet/pqcscan-agent.timer`: `OnCalendar=daily`, `RandomizedDelaySec=2h`; `windows-task.ps1`: 02:00 plus up to 2 h). Under that model 2,000 hosts offer 2,000 / 7,200 s = 0.28 posts/s and 500 hosts 0.07 posts/s. The 500-host test in PR #92 corresponds to about 60,000 daily hosts, 30 times FLEET.md's 2,000-host claim. Item 3 was failed against a load FLEET.md never claims.
- PR #92 bundles carried 300 findings; FLEET-EVIDENCE F19 records that a real scan stores about 1,670.
- The load test never exercised retention: with a 120 s run every host has at most 2 scans and `--keep-scans-per-host` is 12. `prune_host` and `delete_scan` run on every real ingest once a host has 12 scans.
- Two Contradicted claims (FLEET-EVIDENCE rows 71 and 92): FLEET.md Recipes says the state folder grants the task account Modify, but `windows-task.ps1` with a non-SYSTEM `-RunAs` fails at task registration after writing folders, binary, wrapper and `agent.env` (F6, pinned by a Pester test that asserts the failure); FLEET.md Known limits said "Postgres is wired but untested", since corrected to "tested in CI, not profiled".

## Deliverables and acceptance per item

1. **Profile.** `benchmark/fleet/profiles/<db>-before/` and `<db>-after/` for both backends, each with `pyspy.speedscope.json`, `pyspy.raw.txt`, `asyncio-slow.log`, `statements.json` (SQLite: per-statement counts from the trace callback; Postgres: `pg_stat_statements` top 25 and `sum(calls)`), `auto_explain.log.gz` (Postgres), `summary.json` and `summary.md` naming the top three costs by share of sampled wall time and statements per ingest. Each fix commit names the frame it removes; the after-profile shows the frame's share after the fix.
2. **Arrival and capacity.** `run_load.py --rate` (posts/s) with the interval derived, default 1,670 findings per bundle, per-request CSV (`*-requests.csv.gz`) and per-second history next to each result JSON, Little's law fields in every result, two seeds per point. `benchmark/fleet/CAPACITY.md`: method, the sweep table per backend, λ_max with its band (largest rate passing both seeds, smallest failing either), the host-count translations, the herd result, and the reinterpretation of the PR #92 rows.
3. **Overload behaviour.** `INGEST_QUEUE_MAX` in `daemon/fleet.py`, 503 with `Retry-After` past it, a unit test that drives the queue full and sees the 503, the herd run showing 503 counts instead of timeouts, and FLEET.md stating the limits, the capacity and what the collector does past them.
4. **Real soak.** `benchmark/fleet/cloud/soak.sh {up,status,collect,down}` with cloud-init, the sampler cron (`vmstat`, `du`, DB and WAL sizes, row counts, spool depth every 10 minutes), `.github/workflows/soak-windows.yml`, `soak-real.md` rewritten to this procedure, `results/soak-real-day0.json` from `soak --observe`, and the page row stating day 7's date. 5. **Test strength.** `benchmark/fleet/mutation/MUTATION.md` (score, survivors table with verdict per survivor in auth, token bucket, size caps) and `benchmark/fleet/fuzz/FUZZ.md` (CPU-hours, executions, crashes, coverage of `store/bundle.py` from the corpus replay), the corpus committed, `verify` extra extended with `mutmut` and `atheris`.
6. **Contradicted claims.** F6: `windows-task.ps1` either completes a non-SYSTEM `-RunAs` install or refuses it before writing anything; the Pester suite pins the chosen behaviour on both runners; FLEET.md Recipes says which. Postgres: the profile replaces "not profiled"; the pin is the `postgres` and `load-ratio` jobs.
7. **Page.** `docs/FLEET-EVIDENCE.md` gains a one-page section at the top, "Since PR #92", listing each regraded claim with its old and new status and the artefact, the new item verdicts, new findings, and the tag decision with the exact commands a stranger runs to finish item 4 and to reproduce the capacity number.
